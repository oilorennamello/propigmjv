## 1. Which packaging formats are actually sold in Brazil for mayonnaise?

### Takeaway
Snippet-level evidence for mayonnaise in Brazil supports **eight format families**:
1. **Balde** (rigid pail, ~3 kg)
2. **Flexible bulk packs** of 1.0–3 kg. Manufacturers call them "saco", "bag", "pacote", "sachê", "pouch", "refil" or "manga".
3. **Retail flexible pouches** of 180–700 g, which retail calls "sachê"
4. **Sachê de porção** (single-serve sachet, 7–8 g)
5. **Squeeze / bisnaga** (170 g up to 930 g–1 kg)
6. **Pote** in plastic/PET (200 g–1 kg)
7. **Mini-bisnaga** portion units (30 g)
8. **Pote de vidro** (glass jar). This is historical in Brazil (Hellmann's 500 g, 1964). I found no current Brazilian glass SKU.

I found **no** Brazilian evidence of mayonnaise in **galão**, **bag-in-box**, **PC cup / copinho selado**, a **true top-down valve bottle** sold under that name, or **pump/dispenser systems**. All of these show up only as international references.

## 2. Nomenclature: commercial names (PT), English names, technical names; format vs. structure vs. material vs. closure

### Takeaway
Brazilian commercial naming is **unstable and brand-specific**. The same 1–3 kg flexible pack is called "saco" (UFS catalogue), "bag" (UFS product pages, Magalu sellers), "pacote" (Tenda/Vigor), "sachê" (Quero, Vigor, Fugini, Arisco), "pouch"/"refil pouch" (Junior) and "manga" (Vigor). "Sachê" covers everything from 7 g to 2,8 kg. Technically, "doypack" is a **Thimonnier trademark** for a stand-up pouch with a bottom gusset. Use **"stand-up pouch"** as the generic technical term, with **"spouted pouch"** if it has a bico. "Pillow pouch / sachet" is the correct technical term for the flat 7–8 g portion pack.

### Cited Findings
- **Doypack = trademark.** Early stand-up pouch work was done in France by Léon and Louis Doyen. Doyen was president of Thimonnier, which trademarked "Doypack". Dates conflict: 1962 (mirror articles) vs. 1963 (Festo/Thimonnier profile). Patents cited are US 3,502,521 and US 3,935,993 [S] — [Wikipedia – Stand-up pouch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_pouch); [Festo – "Standing strong" (Thimonnier)](https://www.festo.com/us/en/e/blog/in-practice/standing-strong-id_28442); [Packaging Connections – trademark name Doypack](https://www.packagingconnections.com/innovation/you-know-what-trademark-name-stand-pouch-doypack).
- **Pillow pouch** (technical): flexible pack formed from a single web of film with top and bottom transverse seals and a longitudinal back seal (fin or lap). It is the typical structure of single-serve condiment sachets [S] — [Unibo packaging morphology – pillow pouch](https://adu.unibo.it/pack/it/morfologie/pillow-pouch). **Sachet**: small sealed pouch for a single portion, usually multilayer film sealed on 3 or 4 sides. **Stick pack**: slim elongated variant [S] — [Jinlu – stick pack vs sachet](https://www.jinlupacking.com/blogs/stick-pack-vs-sachet-differences/) (supplier blog, low authority). **Flow wrap** is used for "single-serve ketchup, mayonnaise ... packets" in a trade glossary [INT][S] — [PMMI ProSource glossary](https://www.prosource.org/glossary).
- Brazilian usage examples of "**stand up com bico dosador**": Predilecta jam launch (2019) [S] — [Mercado & Consumo](https://www.mercadoeconsumo.com.br/2019/12/12/predilecta-lanca-geleias-em-embalagem-stand-up-com-bico-dosador/). In mayonnaise, "embalagem flexível em PET com bico dosador" (1998) and "embalagem de 1kg em stand up pouch" appear in the Hellmann's timeline (Mauá) [S] — [Mauá resumo](https://maua.br/files/monografias/resumo-historia-embalagem-maionese-brasil-210921.pdf). A separate Mauá monograph covers the history of the stand-up pouch (Cleyton Rebechi de Lima, 2015, 84 pp.) [S] — [Mauá – stand pouch (PDF)](https://maua.br/files/monografias/completo-historia-embalagem_-stand-pouch-210909.pdf).
- **Official manufacturer vocabulary (Unilever Food Solutions, BR)**: "Balde", "Saco", "Bag", "Sachês", "Squeeze" [S] — [UFS 2022](https://www.unileverfoodsolutions.com.br/dam/global-ufs/mcos/sla/brazil/mstb/Catalogo%202022.pdf); [UFS 2025-2](https://www.unileverfoodsolutions.com.br/dam/global-ufs/mcos/sla/brazil/mstb/Catalogo-2025-2.pdf).

- **Retail rigid**: "PET" (Hellmann's/Arisco 500 g), "Pote", "Squeeze/Sqz", "Bisnaga" (Salsaretti, Oderich, Mandoratta, Vigor) [S] — links in §1. "Top Down" appears only in Portugal [INT].
- **Fiscal/EAN shorthand** seen in Systax: "sqz" (squeeze), "sachet", "pta" (in "Kisabor azeitonas pta 200g"; meaning not confirmed, possibly "pote"), "Fs" (foodservice, in "Fs maionese mariana balde 3kg") [S] — [Systax Kisabor](https://systax.com.br/ean/07898937864047/maionese-kisabor-azeitonas-pta-200g); [Systax Mariana](https://www.systax.com.br/ean/07896036092927).
- **Materials (Brazil-specific evidence is thin)**: PET flexible with spout (1998, Hellmann's) [S]; PET/PE → PE//PE mono-material stand-up pouch conversion (Plastrela, pet food, not sauce) [S-prev]; Heinz moved some packs from plastic pots to PET bottles after buying Coniexpress (2011) [S-prev]. Generic buckets: "PP virgem grau alimentício" (not mayo-specific) [S].

### Inferences
- [INF] Suggested **field vocabulary key** (researchers should note the operator's word *and* photograph the pack):
  - *Format* (what it looks like): balde/pail · pote/tub-jar · squeeze-bisnaga/squeeze bottle · stand-up pouch ("doypack") · flat pouch/pillow ("saco/bag") · sachê de porção/portion sachet · mini-bisnaga.
  - *Structure*: rigid injected vs. blow-moulded vs. flexible laminate; gusseted stand-up vs. flat pillow vs. conical "manga".
  - *Material*: read the resin code (PP 5, PEAD/HDPE 2, PET 1, "7/Outros" for laminates).
  - *Closure/dispensing*: lid + tamper seal (lacre); screw cap on spout (bico com tampa rosqueável); flip-top valve; cut-corner/tear notch (picote) with no closure; zipper (zíper).
- [INF] In write-ups, avoid "doypack" as a generic term. Use "stand-up pouch (doypack)" and say whether it has a spout. For the flat 1,6–3 kg UFS "saco", use "flexible bulk bag/pillow pouch (structure to confirm)" until verified.
- [INF] "Sachê" in an operator's mouth can mean the 7 g portion, a 200 g retail pouch, a 1 kg stand-up pouch or a 2,8 kg bag. Always ask for the weight or ask to see it.

### Gaps
- I found no ABRE / EmbalagemMarca / ABIEF glossary with official Portuguese definitions ("sachê almofada", "embalagem tipo travesseiro", "stand-up", "flow pack"). These sites were not returned or reachable.
- No converter (Dixie Toga/Amcor, Packem, Plastrela, Sealed Air) spec for any Brazilian mayonnaise pack was found.
- The Mauá PDFs (history, with figures of every format) could not be opened. Reading them would confirm the structure and materials of the historical formats.
